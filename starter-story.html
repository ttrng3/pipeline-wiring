The honest read

Four things the raw list won't tell you, and they matter more than any single idea below.

01

Your constraint is time, not ideas

You have capital access, a network, and an oversight role that already fills the week. Every idea here dies the same death: you don't give it 15–20 focused hours. That's the real filter, so I weighted bandwidth and buildability heavily.

02

80% of this list is software you can't build

Most entries are horizontal SaaS that need an engineering team and years. For a non-coder, the honest wedge is productized services you can sell now and maybe turn into a tool later — not SaaS from scratch.

03

The edge isn't an idea — it's an arbitrage

Bill in USD, deliver from Hanoi. The same "get-found" or advisory work earns roughly 3–5× more from US/expat clients than VN SMEs, while your cost base stays Vietnamese. That geography trade beats any product pick.

04

Sequence, don't scatter

Services throw off cash in weeks at near-zero capital. Use that cash to fund the one product bet — not your savings. Asset-light, risk-tranched, exactly the structure you already favor. Pick one, not four.

Cross-cutting

Patterns & what to avoid

The moves that repeat

  • Services first, product second. #1–#4 need no build and pay in weeks. #5 is the only real product bet — fund it with service cash, not savings.
  • Niche down hard. rankings.io hit $2.5M/mo by refusing every vertical but one. Every winner here owns a narrow lane; every loser is a generalist.
  • USD in, VND out. The arbitrage is the business. Point any service at US/expat buyers and deliver with Hanoi talent.
  • Distribution before product. The one thing all five require is that you can reach the buyer. You can — real estate, commercial, F&B, US finance. That's the asset, not the code.

What to skip — and why

  • Horizontal SaaS (ConvertKit, Customer.io, Fireflies, Chatbase, Supademo). Great businesses, wrong founder profile — eng teams and years you don't have.
  • The standalone AI-visibility tool (Myzia/BrandAxis as products). The US category is already crowded — a $7.3B-by-2031 projection pulled in dozens of tools. Sell it as a service instead.
  • Pure edtech (Ship30, exam prep). You already killed Sunny. Don't rebuild the same graveyard without a sharper distribution wedge.
  • Physical DTC & concierge (BrüMate, Perfect.live). Capital-heavy or ops-heavy; both torch the bandwidth you can't spare.

What the channel actually teaches

youtube.com/@starterstory — 879K subscribers; the 30 most recent videos run 36K–366K views each. 20 transcripts chosen across playbooks plus the two most-viewed of the quarter. Six more refused transcripts after rate-limiting and are not covered. Every founder number is self-reported on camera.

The five moves that repeat

  • Ideas come from watching money, not having ideas. Comments under viral niche videos (GlowUp, Stella, Go Tall); Sensor Tower top charts then “copy it and make it 10% better” (Prayer Lock, Snag, Gravl, Tomer in Israel); acquire.com listings read as free validation without buying (Go Tall); the most-upvoted forum request (Barn2, $1.7M/yr).
  • Build is not the moat. “Anyone can build an app with Cursor in 2 weeks.” One founder’s app spawned 20 clones in weeks. Moats named on camera: brand, distribution, an owned email list (1.2M Christians), hand-assembled data (AngelMatch, 125K investors), a face people trust.
  • One channel obsessively, then paid on the creative that already worked. Prayer Lock: $10 → $1.88 per trial by putting Spark Ads behind organic videos that passed 10K views. Snag: 100-creator interviews, >50K-view videos become Meta ads. Will: “make a few winning ads and live on them for 4–5 months.”
  • With capital and no audience, buy distribution. $20 UGC clips with idiot-proof scripts (Career Hound); view-guaranteed influencer deals at $2–3 CPM (Early, $50K/mo in 4 months); rev-share with two national influencers after traction so the split is not 50/50 (Tomer, $20K → $80K/mo); a niche YouTuber as equity co-founder to secure the first 10K MRR (Loic).
  • Onboarding and paywall are the product. Lengthening onboarding 5 → 15 minutes tripled conversion; a 91% paywall show rate from a long quiz; hard paywall before sign-in at $440K/mo. Piotr sat at $100/month for two years blaming marketing when the problem was product friction.

What the titles hide

  • Rented reach. GlowUp sold for $150K after TikTok organic went. Jackie Chow’s content sites were wiped by Google AI overviews; an agency and a YouTube audience saved him — “revenue diversification is the only reason I’m on Starter Story today.”
  • Ad-funded thin margins. Letterly: $250K/mo revenue on ~$200K/mo ads plus a 10-person payroll.
  • Shiny objects. Benji abandoned 45 apps before Snag. Loic spent five years on one SaaS with zero users.
  • The channel is a survivorship machine. It sells a course and a community off these stories, interviews the 1 in 100 that worked, and revenue is self-reported. Use it for mechanics, never for base rates.

What you can actually do

Five options, ranked

The constraint that binds every option, from your own ledger: a stranger has paid, or committed in writing. Nothing you have built has passed it, so each option is written so the first milestone is a stranger paying, cheaply, within weeks, before anything is built. The App Intel Engine KILL (22/09) still stands — none of these mines reviews and none is a build factory.

01 · recommended first

Run GlowUp’s step 1 on the salon — no app

Beauty dominates TikTok Shop Vietnam (>$10B GMV in 2025, second only to Shopee — Verified, industry reports). Staff film faceless before/after and process clips; hashtag-mine the VN hair tags exactly as Louis did in #makeup; anchor link → booking or the products the salon already sells. You approve once a week. Gate already passed (strangers pay the salon), faceless so no visibility problem, zero cost, and it makes the one upside line in your portfolio produce data. First milestone: 20 clips in 2 weeks, measure traceable bookings. Kill: none in 30 days.

02

Buy a small cash-flowing app instead of building one

The flipper builds to $10–20K MRR and sells at 2–4× profit. Invert it: buy a $1–5K MRR consumer app on acquire.com with a working paywall and dead marketing, add the distribution the seller never did ($20 UGC, VN-localised ads), hold 6–12 months, relist. An acquired app passes Gate 1 on day one. Capital is the input, not code or hours — fix-and-flip applied to apps. First milestone: a 20-listing spreadsheet, one evening, $0. Risks: US developer and ad accounts in your name; you are buying rented reach unless you diversify it. Council before any LOI.

03

Localise a proven US app to Vietnam

Vietnamese solo developers already sit in today’s VN Lifestyle top-grossing chart: two tử vi apps at #4 and #9 (4,691 and 3,989 ratings), a Vietnamese dating app at #13 with 62,862 ratings (Verified). Pick a huge, US-validated category (calorie scan, height, glow-up, habit lock), ship a Vietnamese version with FlutterFlow or Claude Code, price at VN levels (49–99K₫/month, Assumption), acquire with cheap VN CPMs and one KOL on rev-share after traction. Test first: 10 faceless Vietnamese TikToks about the not-yet-existing app; if comments do not ask for it, do not build. Ceiling: a $10–30K/month business, one Cursor session from being cloned.

04

A paid B2B directory you can assemble by hand

You already run pipelines that collect structured commercial data weekly. A niche database strangers pay $49–97/month for — verified township-scale retail and F&B operators, or foreign-brand franchise holders in Vietnam. Manual first 2,000 rows, programmatic SEO, Stripe. Test first: three pre-orders from LinkedIn/Zalo contacts outside Omni before assembling row one. Why fourth: Rashid’s SEO ramp was 6–8 months, and the subject sits close to your employer’s data.

05 · flagged

Productise a regulated workflow you already master

Savvy Nomad’s model is the best fit to your expertise (compliance, licensing, internal control) and this page already scores “fractional commercial strategist” at 83/100. But it puts your name on a paid service in your exact professional domain while you hold the KSNB/QTRR seat. The Vitals kill was about visibility; this is more visible, not less. Park it until the employment question is decided, or run it only for a non-Vietnamese buyer.

Not this

What I would not do

Rebuild the GlowUp engine literally (US SIM, VPN, seven phones) — it is the part that failed, it breaks the platform’s terms, and it is a full-time job. Anything that starts with a build and ends with “then we market it”. Convening the Dream Team on all five — pick one; the chain manufactures queue when run on a list.

Sequence: two hours this week on option 1 (format sheet in Vietnamese from the hashtags that rank); one evening on option 2’s listing spreadsheet; then a council sitting only on whichever shows a stranger paying.
